# REVIEW: X.md vs Y.md

Both proposals have a clear class identity and a real baseball theme. X is the tighter kit. Y has the better build-depth idea (pitch-type supports) and the best toy-world moments, but it is harder to build and balance.

## Scores (1-10)

| Criterion | X | Y |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Moment-to-moment fun | 7.5 | 7 |
| 2. Simplicity / readability | 7 | 6 |
| 3. Scaling and build depth | 7 | 8 |
| 4. Skill synergy | 8 | 6.5 |
| 5. Baseball authenticity | 8 | 8 |
| 6. Toy-world / It Takes Two fit | 6.5 | 7 |
| 7. Animation and feel | 8 | 7.5 |
| 8. Implementability | 7 | 5.5 |
| **Total (/80)** | **59** | **55.5** |

## X.md

**Strengths**
- The Runners diamond is one resource that carries the whole kit. Generators fill it, spenders cash it in, and Bases Loaded gives Grand Slam a payoff everyone can see.
- Barrel timing (pull, jammed and opposite field, with a fixed window) gives every Strike a skill-based press. The crit, hit-stop, sound and Runner reward all hang off it, which is the main feel strength in either proposal.
- The roles are well covered:
  - Generators: Leadoff Swing, Bunt.
  - Pack clear: Sweeper, Worm Burner, Double Play.
  - Bossing: Pickoff, which rewards reading real telegraphs, plus Squeeze Play and Infield Fly.
  - Movement: Steal Base, Diving Catch, Tag Up.
  - Defence: Check Swing, Bunt, Diving Catch.
- Many skills are real bat actions: Sweeper, Worm Burner, Infield Fly and Squeeze Play.
- The Primed to payoff structure is PoE2-literate.
- The three example builds read cleanly.

**Weaknesses**
- Several non-bat skills feel bolted on (Tag Up, Seventh-Inning Stretch, Diving Catch).
- Tag Up resets everything, which is a balance hazard and a general build crutch.
- Squeeze Play needs a movement skill as its detonator. That is clever but fiddly to read, and it locks the skill to specific partners.
- Infield Fly's culling rules ("not on line drives", "30% on landing") are muddled.
- The toy world is mostly named in passing (sprinklers, marbles, vacuums). Few skills are built from toy objects.
- Line Drive "spends up to 1 Runner" is a weak spender.
- Scaling is described as stat lists. There are no supports or pitch modifiers that change a skill's shape.

## Y.md

**Strengths**
- Pitch types as support gems (Slider, Screwball, Changeup, Fastball) are the best build-depth idea in either file. They are real baseball physics and the native PoE2 idiom.
- The Count / Strikeout (three Strikes on an enemy) is a clever baseball-to-ailment mapping.
- Strong individual skills and moments: Rundown with lawn-gnome fielders, Eephus with a landing circle and a second-hit timing ring, Line Drive with a launch-angle choice, Bat Flip's risk and reward, and Full Swing's Single to Home Run combo.
- It has a better flair and toy fit in places, such as the gnomes and the wind-up pitching machine.
- It is honest about which sources it could not verify.

**Weaknesses**
- It uses four overlapping systems: Hype, Strikes/Count, Runners and dead balls. That is heavy to learn and balance.
- Almost everything is a projectile spender. Line Drive, Curveball, Knuckleball, Double Play, Eephus and Pitching Machine overlap in role. The bat itself is less central.
- Rally Cap is overloaded. It combines the rally cap, the seventh-inning stretch, a cooldown reset and a low-life scaler into one skill.
- Home Run Trot is a movement skill that is a slow jog around a diamond. It is thematically fun but poor as a dodge.
- Knuckleball's randomness conflicts with a "readable at a glance" goal, and Chaos damage is arbitrary.
- Pitching Machine's "Live ball" rule is murky.
- Synergies are mostly "pairs with X" lines. Few are concrete interactions.
- Much of the file is research digest that does not help the designer.
- Defence and mobility are thinner. Slide is the only real dodge, and Bunt and Check Swing are weaker than their X equivalents.

## 3 best skills across both

1. **X: Pickoff.** It punishes a real enemy telegraph with a "caught napping" payoff, and it needs a read rather than a rotation. It is the best boss-fight skill in either file.
2. **Y: Rundown.** Lawn-gnome fielders trap enemies in a pickle. It is baseball, it is toy-world, it is a clear control skill and it is funny.
3. **X: Double Play.** The 6-4-3 chain with fielding numerals floating over targets is readable, authentic and juicy. The Runner refund ties it to Grand Slam.

Honourable mentions: Y's Eephus and Line Drive angle choice, and X's Worm Burner.

## 3 weakest skills across both

1. **Y: Rally Cap.** It is overloaded and incoherent: two baseball ideas, a low-life scaler and a cooldown reset in one.
2. **Y: Knuckleball.** It is random, redundant with Curveball and Line Drive, and a poor fit for a game built on readability.
3. **X: Tag Up.** It is a generic "reset all cooldowns" utility with no bat action, and a balance hazard.

## Verdict

**X is better overall, slightly.** The score gap is 59 to 55.5.

X wins on cohesion, readability, synergy, feel and implementability. A single Runners spine plus Barrel timing means a small team could build and balance it. Y wins on build-depth ideas and on toy-world charm, but its four systems, its projectile-heavy roster and its muddled skills (Rally Cap, Knuckleball, Home Run Trot) drag it down.

The best version would be X's skeleton plus Y's pitch-type supports, Rundown and Eephus.
